SaveBot — a walkthrough of the whole app

A friendly, no-jargon tour of everything SaveBot does — how to use each part and what you'll actually see on screen. Install the real app, try it end to end, and tell me what feels great and what feels off.

SaveBot is your personal place to save posts from Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook and X — and it uses AI to turn each one into a clean, organized card (a recipe, a workout, a travel list, a product, and so on) that you can actually find and use later. Think of it as a "second brain" for the stuff you save but never see again.

1Install it on your Android phone

SaveBot isn't on the Play Store yet, so you'll install it directly from a link. This is normal for test apps — it just takes one extra tap to allow it.

Open this link on the phone you'll test on (tap it right on the Android device, not a computer):

https://expo.dev/accounts/ground-standard-agency/projects/savebot-app/builds/635719a5-8be9-4235-82bf-c187880755eb
  1. On that page, tap Install (or Download) — it downloads a file called an APK (that's just the app).
  2. Open the downloaded file (pull down your notifications, or check Files → Downloads).
  3. Android will likely say "For your security, you can't install unknown apps from this source." Tap Settings → turn on "Allow from this source" → go back → Install. (You can turn it back off after.)
  4. Open SaveBot once it's installed, and allow notifications when it asks — that's how you'll get a "✓ Saved" alert.

2Sign in & quick setup

  1. Create an account or sign in with email or Sign in with Google. Closing and reopening the app should remember you.
  2. On a brand-new account you'll get a few quick questions — what you save (recipes, workouts, travel…), which apps you save from, and how you like things organized.
  3. Answer honestly — SaveBot uses this to arrange your categories so the stuff you care about shows first.

3Save something — the main event

There are two ways in, and the Add tab (the purple + in the middle of the bottom bar) has four modes. Each mode shows its own quick "how-to" steps right on screen.

A. Share straight from another app

  1. Open TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook or X, find a post, and tap its Share button.
  2. Pick SaveBot from the share sheet (tap "More" if you don't see it).
  3. SaveBot catches it and starts analyzing.

B. Add it inside the app (tap the + Add tab)

  • Link — paste any Instagram / TikTok / YouTube / Facebook / X link.
  • Text — paste or type a note, tip, or idea.
  • Image — pick a photo or screenshot; the AI reads any text and describes the picture.
  • Voice — tap the mic and just talk; it turns your words into text you can review, then save.

What you'll see

After you save, a progress pop-up appears: "Analyzing with AI…" with a spinner. It stays until the work is done (keep the app open) — you can't close it early, on purpose. Then it flips to "All done!" with a View button. You'll also get a "✓ Saved" notification, even if you've left the app.

4What the AI gives you

Open any saved item to see its card. Depending on the content, you'll get a tailored layout:

  • Recipe → dish name, time, servings, ingredients, step-by-step.
  • Workout → exercises with sets/reps, equipment, target muscles.
  • Travel → destination, places, tips.
  • Product → name, price, where to buy, pros/cons.
  • Anything else → a clean summary with key points and action items.

Every item also gets a short summary, tags, and a category — filled in for you. Switch between Clean View (the tidy AI version) and Original (the post as it was, with the video playing inline where possible); the app remembers which you prefer.

If you see "AI wasn't fully sure — double-check the details"

That just means the AI had less to work with — usually a photo or video without much caption. The details are still there; it's a gentle nudge to glance over them, not an error.

5Find & organize

  1. Search — type a word from something you saved. Flip on the ✨ Smart toggle to search by meaning (e.g. "quick no-equipment workout") instead of exact words.
  2. Use the filter chips — by category, type, platform, or date.
  3. Browse your categories, switch grid ↔ list, and check the Recent and Favorites tabs.
  4. Manage categories — rename, reorder, hide, or add new ones (and add sub-categories).

6Recipe Cook Mode

  1. Open any recipe and start Cook Mode.
  2. Big kitchen-friendly text, the screen stays awake, step-by-step progression, a per-step timer, and a servings slider that recalculates the ingredients.

7Share & export

  1. On any item, tap the share icon → send a clean summary card to Messages, WhatsApp, email, anywhere. You choose whether to include your private notes.
  2. In the same sheet, tap Export as PDF to save or send a nicely formatted PDF (great for recipes).

8Community — profiles, feed & templates

All under Profile → Community.

  1. Make a save public — open an item and flip the Public toggle. Public saves show up on your profile and in your followers' feed. (Turn your whole profile public first in Privacy & security.)
  2. Find people — search public users and open their profile to Follow.
  3. Following feed — see the saves the people you follow have shared publicly.
  4. Category templates — install a ready-made category setup someone shared, or publish your own setup for others.

9Edit, tidy & archive

  1. On any item: favorite it, edit its notes, tweak tags, and change its category — changes save on their own.
  2. Archive hides an item from your library without deleting it. Find archived items under Profile → Account → Archived items, where you can Restore or permanently Delete them.

10What's not ready yet

So you know what to skip for now — these are coming, not broken:

  • Payments / subscriptions — the last piece we'll build. Everything is free to use in this test.
  • X (Twitter) content — saves work, but the deep text pull-in is best-effort for now.
  • iPhone version — this build is Android; the iOS test build comes next.

How to tell me what you find

For anything that looks off, jot down three things: what you did, what you expected, and what actually happened — a screenshot or screen recording is gold. Small stuff counts too (wording, spacing, anything confusing).
